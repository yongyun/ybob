The New World

A 2026 World Cup special edition that opens a window into soccer for young readers in Taiwan who have never followed the game.

Challenge

More than half of Taiwan's junior high students say they feel real pressure about their future. Many of them are missing something to belong to.

Sport can fill that gap. When Taiwanese athletes won gold at the 2024 Paris Olympics, you could feel the mood of the whole country lift. Soccer is the biggest sport on the planet, yet in Taiwan there's almost no local soccer media and very little Mandarin coverage written for young people. Most teenagers here have never had a reason to watch a single match. This special edition set out to change that.

The cover

The cover is a nod to Michelangelo's The Creation of Adam. In place of the two figures reaching across the Sistine Chapel ceiling, I put Messi and Ronaldo, the two players who defined this generation of soccer, reaching toward the same ball.

Ronaldo sweeps in wrapped in red cloth, Messi rises from a wave of blue, and the title melts down between them. It felt right for a book about a new world to open on a moment of creation.

Concept

The whole book is built around one idea: a window. A window is a way to look out at something new, and a way in.

Window-shaped frames run through every feature. Scan one with your phone and an AR animation of that player comes to life on the page. Opening the magazine becomes the same thing it stands for, a reader literally opening a window into the world of soccer.

I chose stories over statistics. The book follows players like Mohamed Salah, whose success gave Egypt a new sense of itself, and nations like Jordan and Cape Verde reaching their first World Cup. The message is simple: soccer belongs to everyone, no matter how big your country is.

How far
are we?

To make the World Cup feel personal, the book brings it back home. A timeline traces Taiwan's soccer history from 1954 to today, and a game-board style infographic maps every qualifying campaign, the one that always ends in the second round.

It's an honest answer to the question every young fan eventually asks, and the start of a reason to keep watching.

Posters

I also designed a series of player posters. Each one pairs a sharp cutout with motion-blurred layers of the same photo, so the image feels like it's still moving off the page. They're made to end up on a bedroom wall.

On press

The finished book runs 160 pages and covers all 48 national teams. These photos were taken at the print shop during the offset run.

Player
films

Alongside the book, I made a short motion piece for seven of the featured players. Each one takes on the player's own color and number, and uses the same window frames from the magazine to reveal them.
